# Cover Letter Instructions & Anti-AI Humanization Blueprint

The cover letter must begin as pure, normal text. No decorative lines, no horizontal dividers (`---` or `===`), no header elements (`#` or `##`), and no artificial tags.

Strictly format the opening lines as:

## Anti-AI Humanization Directives (Sound Truly Human)
AI-generated cover letters share predictable tropes that recruiters and engineering managers spot immediately. You must strictly avoid these patterns and write with the authentic voice of a skilled builder.

### 1. Ban Robotic Cliché Openings
- **NEVER use**:
  - "I am writing to express my strong interest in..."
  - "I am thrilled / excited / enthusiastic to apply for the position of..."
  - "I was ecstatic to see the opening for..."
  - "I am confident that my skills and background make me the ideal candidate..."
  - "With great interest, I am submitting my application..."
- **DO THIS INSTEAD**:
  - Open directly with a grounded, thoughtful observation about what the company is building, or a direct link between their current engineering challenge and Mario's hands-on experience.
  - Speak with professional composure — like a capable developer who genuinely cares about craft and problem-solving.

### 2. Eliminate Predictable AI Transition Words & Fillers
- **NEVER use**:
  - "Furthermore,"
  - "Moreover,"
  - "In addition,"
  - "Additionally,"
  - "To that end,"
  - "As such,"
  - "Consequently,"
  - "Delving into,"
  - "Spearheaded" / "Leveraged" / "Synergized" (unless describing a very literal, specific technical action).
- **DO THIS INSTEAD**:
  - Connect paragraphs through natural narrative progression:
    - "When we were building the real-time pipeline at..."
    - "On the architectural side,..."
    - "What made this particularly interesting was..."
    - "That experience shaped how I approach..."

### 3. Eliminate AI Symmetrical Rhythm & Sentence Uniformity
- AI models love writing paragraphs of identical length: 3 sentences each, starting with "I [verb]... I also [verb]... By doing so, I [verb]...".
- **DO THIS INSTEAD**:
  - Vary sentence length and density intentionally.
  - Mix concise, direct statements with deeper explanations of technical architecture.
  - Let paragraphs breathe: a short 2-sentence paragraph highlighting a specific focus is far more engaging than uniform blocks of text.

### 4. Remove Artificial Dashes & Excessive Em-Dashes
- Do not pepper em-dashes (`—`) as filler in every sentence to sound "sophisticated".
- Rely on natural punctuation (commas, periods, clean sentence structure).

### 6. Avoid Templated Flattery & Hollow Buzzwords
- Do not fawn over the company with generic superlatives ("your world-renowned culture", "your industry-defining innovation").
- Mention specific, real details from the job description or company context (tech stack, user problem, scaling goal).

### 7. Human, Grounded Sign-Off
- **NEVER use**:
  - "I welcome the opportunity to discuss how my diverse skill set aligns with your esteemed organization."
  - "Thank you in advance for your time and favorable consideration."
- **DO THIS INSTEAD**:
  - Keep the closing grounded and genuine:
    "I would love the opportunity to talk through how my full-stack background and AI workflow experience could contribute to what the team is building at [Company Name]. Thank you for taking the time to read through my application."
  - Sign off cleanly:
    "Sincerely," or "Best regards," followed by:
    "Mario Richie Lim"
